\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\subsubsection{Multi-camera configuration}

A new experimental setup was designed based on the configuration proposed by \cite{Maggi2023},
incorporating modifications aimed at improving measurement repeatability, image quality, and
spatial resolution. A multi-camera system was implemented to simultaneously capture both
global flow patterns and localized high-speed phenomena. Figure~\ref{cameras_distribution}
illustrates the camera distribution around the L-shape and beam containers.

\begin{figure}[htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{Images/SetUpSimple.png}
    \caption{Multi-camera setup for simultaneous PIV and PTV measurements. Cameras 1--3
    provide wide-field coverage of the L-shape container (Cam~1) and beam container from
    orthogonal views (Cams~2--3). Camera~4 captures high-speed close-up footage of the
    L-shape outlet region to resolve initial flow development.}
    \label{cameras_distribution}
\end{figure}

Camera~1 captures the region of interest (ROI) of the L-shape container to track overall
flow progression and fiber distribution, while Cameras~2 and 3 record the beam container
from orthogonal angles to enable stereoscopic reconstruction of three-dimensional fiber
orientations. Camera~4 was positioned to capture a close-up view of the L-shape outlet
at a higher frame rate, resolving the rapid fiber reorientation occurring during the
transition from vertical to horizontal flow. Table~\ref{cameras_specs} summarizes the
specifications of each camera.

All cameras were equipped with 24\,mm $f$/1.8 lenses. The wide aperture ($f$/1.8)
enables short exposure times, reducing motion blur, at the cost of a reduced depth of
field. The shallow depth of field is acceptable because the planar laser sheet for PIV
and the narrow measurement volume for PTV ensure that only features within the focal
plane are illuminated and contribute to the measurements.

\subsubsection{Experimental procedure}

The semi-dilute concentration limit was used as the primary reference for defining the
fiber concentrations. For each Carbopol batch prepared (2\,L total volume), a volume
loss of 198\,mm$^3$ per fiber was estimated, yielding a final suspension volume of
1.802\,L. At this volume, the semi-dilute limit of 0.024\% volume fraction
\cite{Doi1978,Sundararajakumar1997} corresponds to approximately 1023 fibers in the
Carbopol batch. Since the L-shaped device was filled only up to a height of 22.4\,mm,
this translates to approximately 746 fibers effectively deposited within the measurement
domain. Based on this calculation, one fiber concentration below the semi-dilute limit
and two above it were selected, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:fiber_concentrations}.

Fibers were counted by weighing them on a precision balance whose resolution was at
least one order of magnitude smaller than the weight of a single fiber, ensuring
reliable individual fiber counts. The resulting concentrations, expressed in terms of
number of fibers, weight fraction, and volume fraction, are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:fiber_concentrations}.

Each concentration was tested using two Carbopol formulations (0.2\% and 0.5\%),
to assess the sensitivity of the results to batch-to-batch rheological variability.
For each formulation-concentration combination, two independent batches were prepared,
and each batch was used for two experimental runs, yielding a total of 16 runs per
Carbopol type. In experiments with fibers, PTV was performed first to record fiber
dynamics; tracer particles were subsequently added to the same batch to perform PIV
and characterize the velocity field under identical flow conditions. In fiber-free
experiments, two PIV runs were performed per batch. Across all tests per formulation,
this protocol resulted in 10 PIV datasets and 6 PTV datasets per formulation.

Figure~\ref{fig:validationtest} shows representative images of the L-shaped device
prior to each experimental run, illustrating the progressive increase in fiber density
across the tested concentrations. These images confirm the visual distinction between
loading conditions and provide qualitative context for the quantitative tracking results
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:ptv_validation_results}.

\subsection{Carbopol Preparation}

Carbopol Ultrez~10 (Lubrizol) was selected as a transparent viscoplastic analog
for fresh UHPC, following the approach established in prior works
\cite{Dinkgreve2016,Tapia2025}. Its optical transparency allows direct visualization
of fibers and tracer particles, while its stable, time-independent rheology eliminates
the hydration effects that complicate testing with real cementitious materials
\cite{Auernhammer2020}. Two concentrations were prepared: 0.2\% and 0.5\%
(Carbopol/water by mass).

For each batch, deionized water was first measured and its initial pH verified to be
approximately 7.0. The corresponding amount of Carbopol powder was gradually added
over one minute while mixing with a mechanical stirrer at 150\,rpm; stirring was
maintained for one hour to ensure complete and homogeneous polymer dispersion.
Following dispersion, the pH was measured at five points throughout the mixture.
The first dose of 1\,N NaOH solution was then added and the mixture was stirred for
30\,min. After this period the pH was measured again, the second NaOH dose was
added, and stirring continued for an additional 30\,min. A final five-point pH
measurement confirmed that the average value fell within the accepted range. The
specific quantities for each concentration, together with the target pH range, are
summarized in Table~\ref{tab:carbopol_prep}.

The resulting gel was stored in a sealed container at 20\,°C for 24\,h to ensure
complete hydration and structural stabilization. Once stabilized, each batch was
centrifuged in a Hettich Universal~320R centrifuge at 2000\,rpm and 20\,°C for
10\,min to remove entrapped air bubbles, yielding a clear, optically homogeneous
gel suitable for flow visualization \cite{Tapia2025}. Each prepared batch was used
for a maximum of two experimental runs, and the same protocol was applied to both
PIV and PTV measurements.
